About

Advances in visual simulation and generative world models create new opportunities to train autonomous-driving policies through interaction and evaluate their behavior under controlled conditions. However, exploiting these capabilities for safer driving requires more than realistic observations: simulated environments must support meaningful policy learning while exposing failures that emerge as perception, action, and traffic dynamics interact. SafeLoop brings together researchers from computer vision, robot learning, and autonomous driving to examine how simulation and world models can jointly advance policy training and closed-loop safety evaluation.

Six confirmed invited speakers contribute complementary expertise in simulation-based policy learning, world modeling, and visual perception. Two challenge tracks provide concrete settings for diagnosing policy safety failures:

Challenge Track 1

NavSafe-∞ Closed-Loop Safety Challenge

Evaluates responses to safety-critical traffic events. Track details

Challenge Track 2

GS World Adverse-Weather Closed-Loop Challenge

Examines robustness to adverse weather and traffic-scenario edits through world-model-based simulation. Track details

Together, these tracks connect behavioral and visual sources of failure, enabling discussion of what simulated experience teaches a policy and what held-out evaluation reveals about its limitations. Through invited talks and shared failure analyses, SafeLoop aims to establish reproducible evaluation practices and identify how their findings can guide the training of safer driving policies.

Advances in neural rendering and generative world models are expanding the role of simulation in autonomous driving, from replaying recorded scenes to constructing interactive environments for policy learning and evaluation. However, the value of these environments depends on more than visual realism. Because a driving policy changes the states and observations it subsequently encounters, simulation must preserve the cues needed for decision-making and represent the consequences of actions and interactions. This also complicates evaluation: agreement with recorded trajectories alone can miss weaknesses in closed-loop behavior. SafeLoop therefore focuses on three connected questions: which aspects of visual and behavioral fidelity matter for policy learning; how simulation can expose failures under safety-critical interactions and environmental changes; and which evaluation protocols yield reproducible evidence of policy robustness.

Topics

  • Neural rendering and sensor simulation
  • World models for policy learning and evaluation
  • Interactive traffic-agent behavior modeling
  • Reinforcement and imitation learning in simulation
  • Safety-critical scenario generation and coverage
  • Closed-loop evaluation of driving policies
  • Simulation fidelity and safety metrics
  • Robustness to adverse weather and distribution shift
  • Failure analysis and recovery behavior
  • Reproducible benchmarks and evaluation protocols

Outcomes

  • Within four weeks of the workshop, a community report and evaluation checklist covering simulator assumptions, data splits, metrics, and reproducibility.
  • Consented workshop materials, published together with the report.

Challenge Tracks

Two challenge tracks provide concrete settings for diagnosing policy safety failures. NavSafe-∞ evaluates responses to safety-critical traffic events. GS World examines robustness to adverse weather and traffic-scenario edits through world-model-based simulation. Both tracks share one policy interface, one ranking rule, one submission process, and one timeline.

Challenge Track 1

NavSafe-∞ Closed-Loop Safety Challenge

The NavSafe-∞ Closed-Loop Safety Challenge evaluates submitted driving policies in closed loop on the photorealistic safety-critical scenarios of the NavSafe-∞ benchmark. Evaluating all methods on shared scenarios will show which approaches improve collision avoidance, rule compliance, and recovery, and which failure modes persist across policies.

Challenge Track 2

GS World Adverse-Weather Closed-Loop Challenge

The GS World Challenge evaluates visual and control robustness under adverse weather combined with traffic-scenario edits. It tests how adverse weather affects visual and control robustness and the safe handling of edited traffic scenarios.

Dataset

Scenes from Waymo, PAI, and nuScenes cover 13 task types, including vehicle and pedestrian interactions, signal and intersection tasks, constrained-road maneuvers, and special-vehicle encounters. Every task has weather variants, with appearances generated from edited 4DGS renderings via structure-preserving video style transfer.

Both tracks

Rules for both tracks

Policy interface

Input

Rendered camera images, calibration, ego-state history, and a route instruction.

Your policy

Code, weights, and an environment file, run offline on the track hardware.

Output

A future ego trajectory.

Within each track, all entries use the organizer's fixed camera rig, controller, and vehicle model. Participants cannot modify the controller or vehicle model.

Ranking, submission, and integrity

Ranking
Each track ranks entries by mean private-pool Driving Score over three fixed seeds, with its own tie-breakers.
Evaluation specification
Before evaluation opens, each track publishes its exact score formula, termination and timeout rules, inference budget, collision definition, and tie tolerance.
Baselines
Baseline adapters include a complete sample submission and a measured runtime report on the track hardware.
Reporting
Results report scenario-level variation and category or condition breakdowns with the aggregate score. The three seeds measure rollout variation; they are not three independent scenario sets.
Score visibility
Private pools never leave organizer servers. Private-pool scores stay hidden until the deadline.
Submit
Code, weights, an environment file (e.g., Conda or pip), and training-data declarations, together with a 2–4 page extended abstract through OpenReview by the abstract deadline.
Verification
Top entries must pass an independent rerun before awards.
Eligibility
Organizer entries are shown for reference only.
Data licenses
Releases respect source-data and asset licenses. For scenes that cannot be redistributed, the devkit provides acquisition instructions and local processing scripts, or keeps those scenes on the evaluation server. Access to source data does not imply permission to redistribute edited renderings.

If plans change

Evaluation server delay
If an evaluation server is delayed, the organizers rerun submitted packages offline on the private pool.
Data release delay
If dataset preparation or redistribution permissions are delayed, each track releases a license-cleared subset with working baseline adapters on the announced release date and publishes a revised coverage statement.
Evaluation not ready
If a track cannot support its advertised evaluation by March 1, its session presents a clearly labeled benchmark status and baseline failure analysis, without a competitive ranking or awards. Any scope changes are announced to all teams before evaluation opens.
