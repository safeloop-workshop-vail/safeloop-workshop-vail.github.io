About

How can we evaluate and improve safety when a driving policy's actions determine its future observations? SafeLoop brings together vision, robotics, simulation, and transportation researchers around two challenge tracks.

Challenge Track 1

NavSafe-∞ Closed-Loop Safety Challenge

Driving policies are evaluated in closed loop on NavSafe-∞ safety-critical scenarios. Challenge details

Challenge Track 2

World Model Challenge

A planned track on world models. Its scope and evaluation protocol are to be announced. Track details

Submissions are limited to non-archival two-to-four-page extended abstracts that accompany entries in these two challenges. The afternoon program combines six 20-minute keynotes, oral sessions for both challenge tracks, a coffee break, and a panel on evaluation quality. Participants will examine how the choice of simulation environment affects closed-loop safety conclusions.

SafeLoop focuses on evaluating and improving safety through closed-loop interaction between driving policies and their environments. A policy's actions change its future observations, linking perception errors, planning decisions, and recovery behavior. Recent studies expose gaps between offline prediction quality and interactive driving performance, while generative simulation and data-driven evaluation create new opportunities for systematic testing. For the CVPR community, the central question is how visual representations, scene reconstruction, and world models translate into safer driving decisions. The workshop brings together vision, robotics, and transportation researchers to compare methods, diagnose failures, and identify effective interventions.

Topics

The invited talks and discussions welcome perspectives from end-to-end and modular systems, vision-language-action models, and diverse evaluation benchmarks. Topics include:

  • Safety-critical perception and uncertainty
  • Photorealistic and generative simulation
  • World models for closed-loop safety testing
  • Safety-event design and scenario coverage
  • Evaluation metrics and generalization
  • Safe end-to-end and vision-language-action driving
  • Closed-loop learning and adaptation
  • Safe planning and runtime safeguards
  • Recovery and rare-event robustness
  • Reproducible failure analysis and safety assurance

Outcomes

  • Within four weeks of the workshop, a community report and evaluation checklist covering simulator assumptions, data splits, metrics, and reproducibility.
  • Consented workshop materials, published together with the report.

Diversity and Inclusion

Zewei Zhou and Walter Zimmer lead outreach through the vision, robotics, and transportation communities, with particular attention to students and underrepresented researchers. Accessible slides, typed audience questions, and recordings with speaker consent will broaden participation. We will seek student travel support.

The nineteen organizers span eight academic and industrial institutions: UCLA, UCSD, the University of Michigan, TRI, UNT, Toyota InfoTech Labs, KE:SAI, and the University of Tübingen. The team includes students, early-career researchers, senior academics, and industry researchers, connecting computer vision, robot learning, world modeling, and transportation safety.

The invited speakers span academic and industrial research, bringing complementary perspectives on reinforcement learning, simulation, world models, and autonomous-driving perception.

Challenge Track 1

NavSafe-∞ Closed-Loop Safety Challenge

The NavSafe-∞ Closed-Loop Safety Challenge evaluates submitted driving policies in closed loop on the photorealistic safety-critical scenarios of the NavSafe-∞ benchmark. Evaluating all methods on shared scenarios will show which approaches improve collision avoidance, rule compliance, and recovery, and which failure modes persist across policies.

Challenge on Hugging Face NavSafe-∞ project page Paper (arXiv) Devkit: Feb 1, 2027
280public scenarios
28event types in 4 categories
56planned private scenarios, 2 per type
3fixed seeds

Evaluation pools

Both pools cover the same 28 event types in four categories: Traffic Crashes, Vulnerable Road User Crashes, Traffic Violations, and Traffic Incidents.

Released

Public pool

The 280 published scenarios

Released on February 1, 2027, together with the devkit and baseline adapters. Use it to validate your policy and to compare with the 20 policies in the NavSafe-∞ paper.

Used forValidation and comparison

Held by the organizers

Private pool

A planned 56 unreleased scenarios: 28 event types × two scenarios per type

The private pool never leaves organizer servers.

Used forFinal ranking

Policy interface

Input

Rendered camera images, calibration, ego-state history, and a route instruction.

Your policy

Code, weights, and an environment file, run offline on a single NVIDIA RTX 3090 (24 GB).

Output

A future ego trajectory.

All entries share the same camera rig, controller, and vehicle model.

Training data

The NAVSIM/OpenScene training split, and any declared external datasets or pretrained models.

Not provided or not allowed

  • LiDAR
  • Privileged scene state
  • Ground-truth tracks
  • Future trajectories
  • Training on NavSafe data, evaluation logs, or score feedback

Evaluation and ranking

Ranking order

  1. Mean private-pool Driving Score over three fixed seeds
  2. If tied: Success Rate
  3. If still tied: the lowest category score

Reported for every entry

  • Driving Score (DS)
  • Success Rate (SR)
  • Driving Efficiency (DE)
  • Comfort
  • Four category scores
  • Scoring follows the NavSafe-∞ paper.
  • A human driver reference is also evaluated.

Submission and rules

Submit
Code, weights, and an environment file (e.g., Conda or pip) through the Hugging Face portal, together with a 2–4 page extended abstract through OpenReview by the abstract deadline.
Compute
Each submission must run offline on a single NVIDIA RTX 3090 (24 GB).
Limits
One validation submission per day, up to ten in total. Each team chooses one final model.
Score visibility
Private-pool scores stay hidden until the deadline.
Verification
Top entries must pass an independent rerun before awards.
Eligibility
Organizer entries are shown for reference only.
Ethics
Releases respect source-data and asset licenses. Public videos are screened for identifying information. Documentation reports geographic coverage and vulnerable-road-user representation.
